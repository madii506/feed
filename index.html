01  —  The state

One word at the top of every coin, and it is never a badge.

The worst outcome for this product is a buyer who believes a creator is involved when they are not. So the claim state is the first thing on the page, written in words, in the same type as everything else — it reads the same on a phone as it does in a screenshot.

02  —  The claim

Proving the account without asking a platform for permission.

The hard part was never paying a creator. It is proving that the person taking the money is the person the coin is named after — with no email, no identity check, no platform API key, and nothing that lets whoever arrives first walk off with someone else's share.

  1. Connect a wallet on the coin's page.The page issues a claim code bound to that wallet and that account. Nothing is sent anywhere.
  2. Post the code from the account.Anything else in the post is fine. It can be deleted a minute later — it only has to exist once, in public, where anyone can check it.
  3. The code is matched against the account ID.The platform's own immutable number, not the @handle. Handles get sold, renamed and squatted. The number does not.
  4. The escrow opens after the hold.A published waiting period during which the account can cancel the claim with one more post. An account can be taken at 3am and a claim filed at 3:05 — the hold is the window in which the real owner says no.
@yourhandle·the account the coin names

proof of account for my coin — FEED-·······

That is the whole format. Anything else in the post is fine, a reply counts, and deleting it afterwards does not undo the claim.

03  —  The split

Sixty to the feed, forty to us, locked at deploy.

The creator fee is set by the venue the coin launches on, not by us. Whatever arrives, it splits the same way, and both numbers are printed on the coin’s page before the first trade. Not "subject to change", not governed, not adjustable once people have bought — locked, in the sense a creator fee is locked at launch. A split that can move after the buyers arrive is not a split, it is an intention.

04  —  Declining

If a creator says no, we do not keep the money.

A creator can refuse with the same mechanism and a different line. The coin’s page then carries declined permanently, at the top, in the same type size as everything else. The escrowed share goes back to the holders pro rata — and the routed share stops being collected at all.

While the share is live
60%  the feed
40%  FEED
After a decline
40%  FEED
The feed's 60% is not collected by anyone. The fee on that coin is permanently smaller, and our side is exactly the width it was.

That second half is the part worth reading twice. If we absorbed a refusal into our own revenue, every decline would quietly become a payday, and we would have built a machine that earns most from the creators who want nothing to do with it. So the fee drops by the feed's share for the life of the coin.

A decline does not delete the coin. Nobody can un-issue something already onchain, and a pad that claims it can is lying about what a blockchain is. What it can do is make the refusal the loudest thing on the page.

05  —  When it breaks

The failure cases, in the same size type as the happy path.

Every pad publishes the route where everything works. You find out what something actually is from the other list.

It cannotSo it
Make a creator turn upHolds the escrow for twelve months with the countdown on the page, then returns it to holders pro rata. Money sitting in a contract forever with no claimant is not a feature, it is an abandoned balance — and someone eventually writes a proposal to put it to work.
Reach a deleted or banned accountKeeps accruing, refuses the claim, runs the same twelve-month return, and says the account is gone instead of showing posts it cached last Tuesday as though they were live.
Stop two coins existing for one feedShows both on the feed's page, ranked by nothing. No paid slot for whoever launched first, and no coin called official. Exactly one can ever carry claimed, and that is the creator's decision.
Follow a renamed handle by nameBinds to the account ID instead, updates to the new handle, and prints the old one beside it.
Keep reading a closed platform APIEmpties the feed panel and says which platform stopped answering — rather than showing a follower count from last week with no date on it.
Adjudicate a disputed claimPublishes the post, the account ID, the timestamp and the hold window, so the argument is at least conducted over evidence. We have no adjudication power and will not pretend to.

06  —  What is not built

Nothing is live.

No coin has launched, no fee has been collected, no escrow exists and nothing has been claimed.

You will not find a stat rail here reading zero coins, zero creators, zero paid out. Three zeros under a heading called By the numbers is a dashboard impersonating a measurement, and it tells you the truth in the least useful way available. You will not find a quote from a "Creator" either. When someone we can name has actually claimed, we will name them, and the claim post will be linked so you can check it without trusting us.

Working on this page today

  • The readersLink a feed above and the account is resolved live, through this site's own endpoint, against the platform.
  • The claim codeDerived and verified in your browser, from the handle alone. No lookup, nothing stored.
  • The splitArithmetic on a figure you type, beside a SOL price read live.

Not working, because it does not exist

  • The escrow contractNot deployed. The hold period and the twelve-month return are specified above and not yet enforced by anything.
  • LaunchingThe button that mints a coin against a feed is not here. The token does not exist yet: when it does, the address appears on this page and in the pinned post at the same moment, and anyone posting one before that is scamming you.
  • Any claim at allZero creators have claimed, because there is nothing to claim yet. That sentence is the honest version of a counter reading 0.

07  —  The questions

Starting with the one that is least comfortable.

A creator can take the share, refuse it, or never turn up. All three are answered here, and the first question is the one a pad in this category would normally leave out.

Can someone launch a coin for a creator who never agreed?

Yes. That is true of every launchpad that has ever existed, and a pad claiming otherwise is describing a permission system it does not have. What is different here is what happens next: the creator's share accrues to an escrow instead of to whoever launched it, the page says unclaimed in words at the top, and the creator can either take it or refuse it and stop it being collected. We cannot stop a coin being launched about someone. We can stop it quietly earning for a stranger while implying the creator is involved.

What stops me claiming a feed that is not mine?

You would have to post the claim code from the account itself. The code is matched against the platform's account ID, not the @handle, so renaming yourself to match does nothing. If you do control the account — because you stole it — the hold period is the window in which the real owner cancels with one more post.

Why not just verify creators with OAuth or an identity check?

Because both hand a third party a veto over who gets paid. An OAuth app can be revoked by the platform on a Tuesday afternoon, and an identity check turns a launch into a compliance product and excludes most of the people this is for. A public post costs nothing, works on six platforms without asking any of them, and anyone can check it themselves without trusting this site.

Where does the money sit before a claim?

In an escrow tied to that coin's feed, not in our wallet. The contract is not deployed yet, which is stated in section 06 rather than glossed over. When it is, the address goes on every coin page and the balance is readable by anyone without our involvement.

If a creator declines, why not keep their share?

Because that turns every refusal into revenue and makes us the one party who benefits when a creator wants nothing to do with their coin. The fee drops instead. It costs us money, on purpose, and it is the only version of this rule we would be willing to publish before launch rather than after.

Which platforms work?

X, YouTube, TikTok, Instagram, Twitch and Kick. Each one hands over a different amount: some return an account ID to a server and some do not, and where one does not, the coin page says so rather than showing a number we made up. Link a feed at the top of this page and the reader tells you exactly what it got and what it could not get.

Is this endorsed by any of those platforms?

No. None of them have been contacted, none are involved, and none have endorsed anything. Their names appear here because they are where feeds live, the same way a directory lists them.

When is the token?

There is no token yet. When there is, the address appears on this page and in the pinned post at the same moment. Anyone posting one before that is scamming you — which is why that line sits at the top of every page here and will stay there after the mint, with the real address in it.
